# Landing Page Copy Deck
*gregjudelman.com · v1*

Final copy for the landing page sections. Each section shows the structural intent (what each element is, its hierarchy, its position in the section) and the locked copy. Typographic and layout rendering is for the designer to apply from the Greg Judelman Consulting design system.

A few conventions used throughout:
- **Element labels** (Heading, Body, Link, CTA) name what each piece is. The designer chooses type styles from the system.
- **Parenthetical hints** like *(quieter than primary)* indicate relative hierarchy within a section, not absolute specs.
- **Settled structural decisions** (no eyebrows on the landing page, sage 1px rules between sections, no heading on the closing section) are noted inline as locked decisions, not preferences to revisit.
- **Visual element notes** describe register and content, not execution.

Sections are numbered to trace back to `website-v1-outline.md`. Numbers are metadata, not page content. Strategy, rules, and rationale live in the outline. Drafting history lives in `landing-page-copy-v1.md`.

## Section 4.2 — The problem

*Sage 1px rule above the headline as section separator (replaces the eyebrow role used elsewhere in the brand).*

**Headline**

> Where AI adoption gets stuck.

**Body**

> There's a lot of AI activity inside most organizations right now, but most of it stays fragmented. Pilots get launched and prove out something useful, but they never get embedded in how people work day to day. Different teams end up running variations of the same experiment without knowing about each other. Middle managers are being asked to evaluate AI-assisted work without clear standards. And policy frameworks were written for a kind of stability the tools no longer have.
>
> In my experience, most of the harder work in AI adoption sits on the organizational side: decision rights, governance, workflow redesign, evaluation in practice, how learning moves between teams. Vision and pilots are the easier part. The operating model is where it gets stuck.
>
> That's the layer where I work. Senior advisory on strategy, governance, operating model design, and the capacity to use AI well.

## Section 4.3 — How I work

**Body**

> The goal is always the same: capability that stays inside the organization when the engagement ends. The work happens in a rhythm of working sessions where we move through the actual content and challenges together, with time between sessions for synthesis and planning. How involved the team gets depends on what they want and what they have capacity for. The more they engage, the more they own what comes out of it.
>

My focus is on how organizations actually run, where AI fits in, and what needs to change for it to work in operations. When a project needs capabilities outside what I do, like brand, technology, governance infrastructure, or tooling, I bring in collaborators from a network of senior practitioners. We partner on a project-by-project basis.
>
> The practice is evolving in a few adjacent directions, like lab-based engagement models for AI adoption at scale, adaptive governance for complex public-sector organizations, and platforms for coordinating AI work across teams and business units. If any of that connects to what you're working on, write to me.

**Body**

> The AI Transformation Canvas is a strategic design tool for leadership teams. It gives a defined set of categories of strategy and action across the AI transition, with eight blocks organized in three layers: strategic focus, adaptation, and enablement.
