# gregjudelman.com — v1 outline

*Working draft · May 2026*

A recommendation for what the website should be, how it's structured, what the offer architecture looks like, and how it grows. Not the copy yet. Decisions first, then writing.

---

## 1. What this site is for

A single goal: turn the right kind of attention into the right kind of conversation. The right attention is a senior buyer in government, health, university, foundation, or regulated enterprise who is already navigating an AI transformation problem and recognizes the middle layer when they see it named. The right conversation is a 30-minute intro call where the buyer arrives with a specific situation and you arrive with a specific way to think about it.

Everything on the site serves that goal. If a section doesn't move a reader toward booking a call, downloading the canvas, or taking the diagnostic, it doesn't go on v1.

## 2. Masthead and positioning

The wordmark stays as your name. The positioning line is held to the simplest form that does the work: a single "Senior advisory for…" phrase that names who and what, with no tagline rhythm or observation-as-headline construction. The framework detail (governance, operating model, capability) moves to the supporting line.

Four simple options. The variable is mostly the word for the work itself — *AI*, *AI adoption*, *the AI transition* — and the presence of *leaders working on*.

**Option A — With "leaders working on" + transition (recommended)**

> Greg Judelman
> Senior advisor for leaders working on the human and organizational side of the AI transition.
> *Strategy, governance, operating models, and the organizational capacity behind AI adoption.*

Why it works: simple, plainspoken, and uses your own framing. *The AI transition* is more honest than *transformation*, and it's a phrase you actually use. *Leaders working on* pre-qualifies who the page is for without doing it heavy-handedly. The supporting line carries the framework specifics.

**Option B — Shorter, no "transition"**

> Greg Judelman
> Senior advisory for leaders working on the human and organizational side of AI.
> *Strategy, governance, operating models, and the organizational capacity behind AI adoption.*

Why it works: tighter. *AI* is broader than *the AI transition*, so the supporting line works harder to specify the scope. Closer to what you typed in your message.

**Option C — Service-frame, no "leaders"**

> Greg Judelman
> Senior advisory on the human and organizational side of AI adoption.
> *Strategy, governance, operating models, and the capacity to use AI well.*

Why it works: shortest and least directive about audience. Works if you want the line to read more as a description of the practice and less as an invitation to a specific reader.

**Option D — Work-frame**

> Greg Judelman
> Senior advisory for the human and organizational work of AI adoption.
> *Strategy, governance, operating models, and the capacity to use AI well.*

Why it works: leads with the *work*, not the *leaders*. Slightly more abstract than A or B, slightly more confident than C.

**My recommendation: Option A.** It's the most complete sentence — names who, what, and the actual scope — and it uses *transition* rather than *transformation*, which is more accurate and more yours. If Option A feels overlong, Option B is the simpler version that keeps the same core.

Across all options, the visual treatment is the same: name in Source Serif 4 Semibold, hero line in Source Serif 4 Regular at display size, supporting line in Helvetica Neue Light. No eyebrow on the hero. Eyebrows reappear on subsequent sections as signposts.

## 4. Landing page outline

Nine sections, in this order. Each one earns its place by doing a job the others don't.

### 4.1 Hero

Wordmark, positioning line, one supporting sentence, one CTA.

**Job:** Tell a senior buyer within four seconds: who you are, what kind of work, and whether to keep scrolling.

**Direction (not final copy):**

- Positioning line as above.
- One sentence under it that names the middle layer without using the phrase yet. Example direction: *"Strategy, governance, and operating models for organizations adopting AI under real constraints."*
- Primary CTA: *Book a 30-minute call.*
- Secondary CTA (small): *Take the diagnostic.*

No hero image. The visual brand's restraint and typography carry it. Calm, paper or ink surface, generous whitespace.

### 4.2 The problem you're actually solving

A short framed observation, written in the voice of a buyer's reality, not your service categories. This is the section where the senior buyer has to recognize their actual situation. The voice register: observational, descriptive, slightly conversational. Long sentences with conjunctions are good. Compressed sentence fragments are not. The pain lands because it's accurate, not because it's amplified.

**Job:** The senior buyer reads through the section and decides this person sees what they see.

**Direction:** A short headline followed by three short paragraphs. No eyebrow (see Section 8 and Section 10).

Final copy:

**Headline:**

> Where AI adoption gets stuck.

**Body:**

> There's a lot of AI activity inside most organizations right now, but most of it stays fragmented. Pilots get launched and prove out something useful, but they never get embedded in how people work day to day. Different teams end up running variations of the same experiment without knowing about each other. Middle managers are being asked to evaluate AI-assisted work without clear standards. And policy frameworks were written for a kind of stability the tools no longer have.
> 
> In my experience, most of the harder work in AI adoption sits on the organizational side: decision rights, governance, workflow redesign, evaluation in practice, how learning moves between teams. Vision and pilots are the easier part. The operating model is where it gets stuck.
> 
> That's the layer where I work. Senior advisory on strategy, governance, operating model design, and the capacity to use AI well.

**Rules:**

- No urgency language. No fear framing.
- No *X, not Y* constructions, no *rarely X. It's Y* structures. Describe what is, not what isn't.
- No aphoristic closers trying to be quotable.
- Longer descriptive sentences with conjunctions (*and, but, which*) are preferred to compressed sentence fragments.
- Hedge words (*fairly, about, really, usually, tends to*) get cut unless they're doing real work. *In my experience* and *actually* stay because they're voice signatures.
- "AI transition" rather than "AI transformation" in the body copy. The Canvas keeps its name (see Appendix A).
- The headline *Where AI adoption gets stuck* is plainly descriptive. It names the section's topic without trying to be quotable, and it pairs with the section's argument (AI adoption stalls on the organizational layer).

### 4.3 How I work

Three paragraphs: methodology (iterative practice and participation), heritage and network, and a short forward-direction signal. The senior-advisory positioning paragraph that used to lead this section has been cut — it's already established by the hero and the problem section, and restating it here was redundant.

**Job:** Describe how the work is actually done, what the practice grew out of, and where it's evolving. The reader who recognizes the iterative-practice methodology and the service-design heritage as kindred orientation will self-identify.

**Direction:** Three short paragraphs. Eyebrow `PRACTICE` over the section.

Final copy:

> The goal is always the same: capability that stays inside the organization when the engagement ends. The work happens in a rhythm of working sessions where we move through the actual content and challenges together, with time between sessions for synthesis and planning. How involved the team gets depends on what they want and what they have capacity for. The more they engage, the more they own what comes out of it.
> 
> I've spent twenty years in service design, experience design, and organizational innovation work, now applied to the AI transition. My focus is on how organizations actually run, where AI fits in, and what needs to change for it to work in operations. When a project needs capabilities outside what I do, like brand, technology, governance infrastructure, or tooling, I bring in collaborators from a network of senior practitioners. We partner on a project-by-project basis.
> 
> The practice is evolving in a few adjacent directions, like lab-based engagement models for AI adoption at scale, adaptive governance for complex public-sector organizations, and platforms for coordinating AI work across teams and business units. If any of that connects to what you're working on, write to me.

**Rules:**

- The positioning paragraph (*senior advisory, not project delivery at scale; judgment and depth, not methodology and volume*) is deliberately cut. The hero already establishes senior advisory. Restating it here ran two paired *X, not Y* constructions back-to-back, which is a banned pattern in the brand voice doc.
- The *methodology* paragraph carries the iterative-practice idea in plain operational language. *Working sessions, time on my side, synthesize, plan, document.* No agile or lean-startup jargon (*build-measure-learn*, etc.). The principle that participation builds capability is stated positively; the deck-handoff alternative is implied without being named.
- The *network* sentence at the end of the heritage paragraph addresses the "is one person enough?" question. Four specialist domains named (brand, technology, governance infrastructure, tooling). Project-by-project framing is honest and doesn't oversell a fixed bench.
- Service design is named as the lens, not as a separate offering. The reader should understand it as the *why* behind the focus, not a second practice.
- "Service design, experience design, and organizational innovation" is the right phrase. Specific enough to mean something. Doesn't get into UI/UX or product design (which would muddy the position).
- Business model design, value proposition design, and product sensibility don't get named on v1. They show up implicitly in how the conversation unfolds with a client. The site doesn't need to list them.
- The third paragraph carries the forward-direction signal that an Explorations section would have carried, but distributed lightly. No collaborators, clients, or project names. Just three directions stated at a high level, with an open invitation. Distinct enough that the right reader recognizes themselves; vague enough that it doesn't attract exploratory inbound.

**Direction:**

## 5. Offer architecture

Four tiers. Named clearly, scoped by duration and format, with the buying decision obvious at each step. Pricing is deliberately not on the page in v1 (see Section 10 for the reasoning). Scope and format do enough qualification work without filtering out conversations you'd actually want.

### Tier 0 — Free entry points (lead magnets)

These sit upstream of any paid engagement. Their job is to give a senior buyer a useful artifact in exchange for an email and a clearer sense of whether to talk.

| Asset                        | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                              | What the buyer gets                                 | What you get                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **AI Transformation Canvas** | Downloadable PDF of the canvas plus a short accompanying deck (5–7 slides) explaining the eight blocks and how to use it in a working session                                                                           | A real tool they can take into a leadership meeting | Email, signal of intent (downloaded canvas), permission to follow up                         |
| **AI Transition Diagnostic** | Form-based questionnaire (Typeform or similar to start), eight blocks, four tiers each, ~10 minutes. Returns a profile with current tier per block, a one-line interpretation per block, and three suggested next moves | A personalized snapshot of where they are           | Email, a much richer sales asset for the intro call: you both arrive having seen the profile |
| **30-minute intro call**     | Free conversation, scoped: what they're working on, what's stuck, what would help                                                                                                                                       | Direct access to a senior advisor, no pitch         | Qualification, scoping signal, a real assessment of fit                                      |

### Tier 1 — Briefing session

**Scope:** 60–90 minutes with a leadership team. Virtual or in-person.
**What it is:** A structured working session. You bring the Canvas, walk the team through the eight blocks, surface the live tensions in their organization. Outputs: a populated canvas, a short read-back of what's blocking progress, three named next moves.
**For whom:** Leadership teams who want a fast, expert-led conversation before committing to scoped work.
**Position on the page as:** *"A focused first session for leadership teams who want to align around AI before scoping deeper work."*

### Tier 2 — Half-day or full-day workshop

**Scope:** 3–4 hour or full-day working session with the leadership team or extended group.
**What it is:** The full Canvas workshop sequence. Eight phases, owner-mandatory, current-tension surfaced per block, closing read-back. Builds the diagnostic *with* the team rather than handing them a result. Produces a populated wall canvas, a written synthesis, and a sequenced set of next moves with named owners.
**For whom:** Organizations who know they need to align around AI transformation but don't yet have a shared picture.
**Position on the page as:** *"A working session that gives leadership teams shared language and a coordinated next move."*

### Tier 3 — Advisory engagement

**Scope:** Multi-week or multi-month engagement. Sized to the problem.
**What it is:** Three sub-shapes, each with its own scope:

- *AI Transition Assessment* (typically 4–6 weeks). Current-state read across the eight blocks of the Canvas, prioritized roadmap, operating model recommendations.
- *Operating Model Design* (typically 8–12 weeks). Governance, evaluation, roles, cadence, decision rights. The middle-layer work, scoped.
- *Senior Advisory Retainer* (3–6 months). Standing advisory presence, not project delivery. For organizations who need ongoing judgment, not staffed work.

**For whom:** Organizations ready to do the work, with funding and authority in place.
**Position on the page as:** *"For organizations committed to building real AI operating capacity, not just running pilots."*

### How this reads on the page

A three-column structure under the *How to work together* heading. No eyebrows (per Section 10). Each card carries:

- Card name in Source Serif 4 Semibold (e.g. *Briefing session*)
- Scope line in Helvetica Neue Light Italic, smaller (duration · audience)
- One-sentence description in Helvetica Neue Light
- Single CTA in sage: *Book a call →*

The intro paragraph above the cards names the range and the entry point in two sentences. The 30-minute call is named there rather than sitting as a fourth, lighter-weight option, since the same *Book a call* CTA appears on every card and every path leads to the same conversation.

Final copy:

**Heading:**

> How to work together.

**Intro paragraph:**

> There are a few ways to work together, from a single working session to multi-month advisory engagements. Every option starts with a 30-minute call to talk through what you're working on.

**Card 1 — Briefing session:**

> *60–90 minutes · leadership team*
> 
> A working session for thinking through a specific question together. Could be a Canvas-based map of where the organization is, an orientation on AI governance, AI in service and experience design, or another topic. The team leaves with a shared view and a clearer sense of what to focus on next.

**Card 2 — Workshop:**

## 6. Lead magnet flow

How the canvas and diagnostic actually function as a system:

1. **Canvas download.** Visitor lands on `/canvas`, sees a preview, gives email, gets the PDF and short deck. Sequence: confirmation email immediately, then a single follow-up email two weeks later asking if they used it and offering a conversation.

2. **Diagnostic.** Visitor lands on `/diagnostic`, takes the questionnaire, sees their profile on screen. Email is required to receive the PDF version of the profile. Sequence: profile email immediately (PDF attached), then a single follow-up one week later: *"Happy to walk you through your profile if useful. 30 minutes, no commitment."*

3. **Intro call.** Booked through Calendly. Confirmation includes one short paragraph: *"To make the most of this, bring one specific situation you're working on. If you've taken the diagnostic, share the profile and we'll start there."*

The diagnostic does double duty: it qualifies the lead (anyone whose blocks come in at Tier 0 across the board isn't ready), and it pre-frames the conversation so the call doesn't start from zero.

## 7. Where it grows

Five expansion slots, each one a small addition rather than a restructure:

**Writing.** `/writing` becomes active when there are 6–8 posts or essays worth surfacing. Pull from LinkedIn and Substack. Editorial layout, sage rule between cards, eyebrow with date. No comments, no engagement metrics.

**Case studies.** A `/cases` route, added when you have one or two write-ups that have been cleared with clients. Each case study follows a fixed template: situation, what was actually hard, how the work was structured, what changed, what's still open. Editorial register, not marketing.

**ReGenAI.** For v1, one line on the landing page (in the *Where I've worked* section) with a direct contact invitation, not a sub-route. If a dedicated ReGenAI website goes live, the line becomes a link. The intent is to signal a values orientation without pulling the consulting site into ReGenAI's longer-horizon register.

**Speaking and convening.** Add when there's a real schedule worth publishing. Until then, it lives inside the *Where I've worked* section as a line.

**Practice notes / resources.** A small library page for downloadable artifacts (the canvas, eventually a facilitator's guide, sample evaluation rubrics). Sits under `/canvas` initially, breaks out into its own route when there are three or more assets.

## 8. Visual treatment notes

The site should feel like the visual brand: research publication, not consultancy template. Specific decisions:

- **Default surface:** paper (`#F6F2EC`). Reserve ink for the hero section and one or two contrast moments. Don't dark-mode the whole site.
- **Type:** Source Serif 4 Semibold for headings, Helvetica Neue Light for body. Same scale system as the visual brand.
- **No eyebrows on the landing page.** Section headlines do the wayfinding. Eyebrows are part of the broader visual brand and stay available for documents, decks, and the `/canvas` page, where there's enough density to make them earn their keep. On the landing page, where each section is short and the headline already names the topic, eyebrows would either repeat the headline or attempt categorical labels that don't add orientation. See Section 10 for the settled decision.
- **Section separators:** sage 1px horizontal rule above each section's heading. This carries the sage punctuation that the eyebrows would have provided, while letting the headlines stand alone.
- **Square corners everywhere.** No SaaS cards.
- **The Canvas preview** is the only diagram on the landing page. Render it carefully, in the paper-surface visual language.
- **No icons** except where functional. No illustrations. No stock photography. No headshot in the hero (a small portrait can sit in the *Where I've worked* section if you want, but it's optional).
- **Single accent:** sage (`#7DA7A7`), used for the 1px section rules and one or two key emphases per page.

## Appendix A — Vocabulary system: *transformation* vs *transition*

A clear split between proper-noun brand vocabulary and descriptive body copy. The Canvas is the established IP and keeps its name. Services built on the Canvas use *transition* to align with the value prop language and to avoid the market bloat around *AI transformation*.

| Term                          | Type                         | Where it appears                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **AI Transformation Canvas**  | Proper noun (IP / framework) | The `/canvas` page, references in offer descriptions, the deliverable itself. Established, keep. |
| **AI Transition Diagnostic**  | Proper noun (lead magnet)    | Section 4.5, the `/diagnostic` route, the form itself, the resulting profile.                    |

| *the AI transition*           | Descriptive                  | Hero supporting line, Section 4.2 problem framing, body copy throughout, value prop statements.  |
| *AI transformation* (general) | Avoid in body copy           | Reserve only for the Canvas's name. Don't use as a generic verb or category descriptor.          |

**The logic:** the Canvas is the named instrument; services *use* the Canvas to support a client's AI transition. Naming the Canvas with *Transformation* preserves the established IP and the existing audience's recognition. Naming the services with *Transition* matches the value prop and signals a sharper position than the bloated *AI transformation* category language.

**Internal phrasing test.** A sentence like *"The AI Transition Assessment is a 4–6 week engagement that uses the AI Transformation Canvas to map your current state and a path forward."* is the model usage: proper nouns capitalized, descriptive verb in the *transition* register. If a draft contains *"we provide AI transformation services"* or *"AI transformation strategy"* as generic descriptors, rewrite. Reserve *transformation* for the Canvas's name only.

**The deeper point underneath.** Your actual practice is closer to *organizational adaptation under conditions created by AI* than it is to *AI transformation* in the McKinsey sense. The body copy doesn't need to use that phrasing literally, but the reader should feel the orientation. Section 4.2 and the supporting line under the masthead carry it: *the human and organizational side of the AI transition*. Hold that line.
